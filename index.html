Why this matters now

New provincial rules. Small offices. Real deadlines.

Alberta's Protection of Privacy Act (POPA) and the updated Access to Information Act (ATIA) are changing how every municipality handles records and privacy requests. Big cities have compliance teams. Villages have an office administrator wearing five hats.

Privacy requests have deadlines

Residents can request their personal information, and the response clock is measured in business days. Miss the process, and small offices feel it most.

Written programs are expected

A privacy management program, a public information bank inventory, and retention schedules aren't just for big cities anymore.

Budgets are being written right now

Villages are deciding their 2027 budgets this fall. Getting compliant is far cheaper as a planned line item than as an emergency.

The official bits: ATIA s.13(1) provides a 30-business-day starting rule for access requests. POPA s.7 governs corrections. POPA s.25 covers privacy management programs. This page explains my services in plain language — your municipality's legal advisor confirms what applies to you.

Questions councils actually ask

Straight answers

Is this legal advice?

No — and anyone who tells you a website or spreadsheet is legal advice is selling you something. I provide practical privacy-program, records, and technology work based on the published legislation and guidance. Your municipality's legal counsel confirms what applies to your situation, and I'm glad to work alongside them.

We're tiny — are we too small for this?

There's no such thing. POPA and ATIA apply regardless of population. Small villages actually benefit most: the work is the same, but my fixed prices are built for small budgets. Villages under 500 people are exactly who I built this business for.

What does the free assessment involve?

I visit your office (or we meet by video), spend about an hour learning how you handle records and requests today, and hand you a written gap summary — free, no obligation. Even if you never hire me, you'll know where you stand. That's worth an hour.

Why are you so much cheaper than the big firms?

Low overhead, not low quality. No downtown office, no partners to feed, no junior staff learning on your file. You pay for the work itself. I also know where villages actually get stuck — and I build tools (including working software) that make the routine parts fast.

How long does the work take?

A website typically takes 2–3 weeks. The Privacy & Records Package is usually done within a month of the assessment. I'll give you a written timeline before we start, and I keep you posted along the way.

What if we already have some of this in place?

Great — you only pay for what you need. The free assessment figures out exactly where the gaps are, and the written scope reflects that. I won't sell you work you've already done.
